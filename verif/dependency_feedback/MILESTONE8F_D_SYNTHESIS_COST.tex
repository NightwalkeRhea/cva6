\subsubsection*{Milestone 8F-D: Cost of the integrated monitor}

I measured the monitor cost using a paired synthesis experiment for the
\texttt{cv32a65x} configuration. The baseline contains four RAW dependency
observations: RS1 and RS2 for each of the two issue ports. All four observations
use the same eight-entry scoreboard.

The monitored design contains exactly the same four RAW observations and adds
one shared five-bit sticky monitor. The monitor consumes the match vectors
already calculated by the RAW checkers, so it does not duplicate the register
comparators.

I synthesized both designs with the same Yosys version, optimization passes,
and generic-cell mapping:

\begin{verbatim}
yosys -Q \
  -l fi-results/milestone8fd-synthesis/baseline.log \
  verif/dependency_feedback/scripts/\
synth_dependency_feedback_cv32a65x_baseline.ys

yosys -Q \
  -l fi-results/milestone8fd-synthesis/monitored.log \
  verif/dependency_feedback/scripts/\
synth_dependency_feedback_cv32a65x_monitored.ys
\end{verbatim}

The synthesis results were:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrrr}
\hline
Metric & Baseline & Monitored & Delta & Delta percentage \\
\hline
Wires               & 859  & 1050 & +191 & +22.24\% \\
Wire bits           & 1278 & 1568 & +290 & +22.69\% \\
Hardware cells      & 823  & 1005 & +182 & +22.11\% \\
Combinational cells & 823  & 1000 & +177 & +21.51\% \\
State cells         & 0    & 5    & +5   & N/A \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The hardware-cell counts exclude Yosys \texttt{\$scopeinfo} metadata because
it does not represent physical hardware. Both netlists passed the Yosys
structural check with zero reported problems.

The monitored netlist contains exactly five \texttt{\$\_DFF\_PN0\_} cells.
This confirms that every sticky feedback bit survived synthesis and was not
optimized away.

\subsubsection*{Non-interference proof}

I also created a formal paired wrapper that drives the baseline and monitored
RAW cones with identical symbolic inputs. I asked Yosys SAT to prove that all
four RAW-valid outputs and all four selected-producer indices remain identical.

The result was:

\begin{verbatim}
SAT proof finished - no model found: SUCCESS!
\end{verbatim}

Therefore, adding the feedback monitor does not change the dependency decisions
produced by the modeled RAW-checker boundary.

\subsubsection*{Structural-depth limitation}

The generic baseline netlist has a longest topological path of 15 logic levels,
ending at a producer-index output. The monitored netlist has a 25-level path
ending at a feedback-register input. The additional path belongs to the
observational monitor and does not feed an architectural RAW output.

I do not interpret these numbers as a timing or frequency result. This
open-source flow does not use a target standard-cell timing library, placement,
routing, or extracted fanout. A credible critical-path comparison must be
performed later using the target technology or the commercial synthesis flow.

\subsubsection*{Result}

The real dual-issue monitor boundary adds 182 generic hardware cells, consisting
of 177 combinational cells and five state cells. The reported 22.11\% increase
is relative only to the small four-checker RAW cone. It is not a 22.11\%
increase in the area of the complete CVA6 processor.

This experiment shows that the integrated monitor is synthesizable, that its
state survives optimization, that it reuses existing dependency comparisons,
and that it remains observational with respect to RAW decisions. The next step
is therefore the controlled MicroGP experiment with and without the five-bit
feedback under the same generation and fault budgets.
